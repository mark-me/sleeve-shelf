# Your shelves

## 🧱 Layout

**Layout** shows all your cabinets with their shelves and what stands on them. Neighbouring albums of one artist form a **block**; the number on a block is how many albums it holds — click it to split the block into single albums. A bar on each shelf shows how full it is.

When your cabinets are spread over rooms, each room gets a tab. *Go to cabinet…* at the top stays in view while you scroll and jumps to any cabinet, or to the albums not on a shelf.

## ✋ Dragging in Layout

Drag a block to another spot on the same shelf, or to another shelf. Every move is saved at once, and a version of the layout from before you started is kept.

On a phone, **hold a block for a moment** until it lifts, then drag it. A quick swipe that starts on a block scrolls the page instead, so you don't move albums by accident.

For a shelf far away, use the ⋮ on a block (or right-click it) and pick the shelf from the list: the block goes to the end of that shelf, and the page jumps there so you can drag it to its exact spot.

## 🚚 Move

**Move** (under Shelves) moves more than one block at a time: an album, an artist, a family, or a whole cluster. The pages of an album, artist and cluster, and each family, have a **Move…** button that opens it with that already chosen.

1. **What** — search for it, or come from its page. Of an artist, family or cluster the albums that stand on a shelf go, in sorting order; what stands in a showcase or a cabinet kept outside the sorting stays there.
2. **Where to** — one of three:
    - **Where it belongs**: next to what each album goes with, on your shelves as they stand — the artist's other albums, the artists of its era in its cluster, or its cluster. A location rule is kept to. Handy to put back something you moved before, without a whole proposal. An album whose artist has no cluster stays where it is.
    - **At the front of a shelf**: it runs on over the next shelves of that cabinet, and what stood there moves up behind it. A shelf counts as at least as wide as what stands on it now, so an estimated width that is too narrow doesn't push albums on. What no longer fits stays at the end of the cabinet's last shelf.
    - **At the end of a shelf**: after what stands there; nothing else moves. Choose *Not on a shelf* to take it off the shelves.
3. **What changes** — every shelf that changes, with its albums now and after and how full it gets; a shelf that ends up over its width is marked. Only **Move** changes anything, and the layout from before is kept as a version.

To put albums in their exact spot on a shelf, drag them in Layout afterwards.

## 🗄️ Cabinets and shelves

**Cabinets** describes your storage: each cabinet with its room and its shelves, in the order you walk them — the order Browse follows and a proposal fills. The arrows change that order. A shelf has a name, a width, and optionally a type (top- or front-loader), a row (top or bottom), and a reachability score.

A cabinet can be **kept outside the sorting** — for records set aside, storage elsewhere, or a case you are still going to buy. What stands there stays, and a proposal adds nothing to it.

A shelf can only be removed once it is empty, and a cabinet once it has no shelves, so nothing ever loses its place by tidying up.

## 📏 Measured or estimated widths

How many records fit on a shelf depends on its width. Widths taken from a workbook are **estimates**, shown as ≈ — they tend to come out too narrow. Once you have measured a shelf, enter its width and tick **I measured this width**.

Only a measured shelf that is too full is marked in red and counted as waiting on the Dashboard. A shelf over its estimated width is shown in grey: it mostly says the shelf needs measuring.

Album widths are estimated from their format (see [Settings](settings.md)). Box sets and bundles vary too much for that; measure them under **Cabinets → Album widths**.

## 🪟 Showcase

A **showcase** is a top-loader that holds a sample of the artists you have most of, facing front. Mark a top-loader as a showcase under Cabinets; the albums standing on it become its sample. The Showcase screen shows, per artist, how many of their albums are on display, and lets you **exchange** one for another: each album takes the other's spot, so the top-loader and the shelf hold as many albums as before.

A proposal leaves a showcase exactly as it is.

## ⏪ Versions

Every layout you had can be brought back. A version is saved when you load a workbook, when you accept a proposal, before you move albums by hand (at most once an hour), and whenever you choose to. **Put back** restores where your albums stood — nothing else — after saving the layout you have now, so you can always return.
